# AI Advent — My Review After Seven Weeks

**This review is ready to publish!** 😁

At the beginning of the challenge, we were told: “Warn your significant others that you’ll be busy for the next seven weeks.”

Well, that turned out to be absolutely true. Especially from week five onward.)))

I would suggest adding a small note to that warning: **this is not a joke!** 😂

## Where I started

When I joined the challenge, my AI experience was mostly limited to writing prompts in a browser.

And I thought it was pretty cool when AI responded with what felt like understanding and emotion — almost like talking to a real conversation partner.

Seven weeks later, I understand much better how those effects are produced. There is no magic involved. By experimenting with models, prompts, and settings, you can create surprisingly similar experiences even on your own local machine.

But the biggest change was in how I think about AI.

**Before the course, I asked AI questions. Now I can give it a task, provide tools, organize the workflow, and verify the result.**

That is a completely different level of interaction.

## What I built

I now have my own home AI assistant running on my hardware and accessible to my family from different devices.

It is not just a five-minute course demo — it is a working system that we can use and keep improving. There are still a few things I want to fine-tune, but that can wait until after Advanced.))

And I did not need an expensive server to get started. The core system runs locally on my MacBook Pro M1 with 32 GB of memory.

I also built some practical AI agents:

- **My first code review agent took about three hours.**
- **An agent that maintains a daily development journal and generates documentation from merged pull requests took about five hours.**

Previously, projects like these seemed like major standalone undertakings. Now I know how to approach them and build something useful in a relatively short time.

## Deadlines, perfectionism, and knowing when to stop

The weekly submission deadlines were tough — and absolutely necessary.

Without them, I probably would have disappeared into endless quality improvements.

I especially “enjoyed” the final week.)))

By Friday, I still had not finished Day 31. My ChatGPT limits were running out, and progress was painfully slow.

That was when I realized that maximizing quality was no longer a viable strategy.

According to an AI estimate, fully completing everything I had in mind would have required another **10–35 hours**.

So I reassessed the priorities, defined the minimum acceptance criteria, and focused on finishing the work.

This revealed an interesting paradox: **AI speeds up development, but it can also suggest so many improvements that the work becomes practically endless.**

Knowing when to say “that's enough; the task is done” turned out to be just as important as knowing how to work with AI.

Perfectionism is wonderful. Just be realistic about your time and energy.))

In the end, I completed all 35 days by the deadline. That did not mean pretending every extended quality check was perfect: sometimes a documented, limited result was the right decision.

## Do you have to know how to program?

**I'm a Kotlin Multik developer** — Kotlin Multiplatform, if we want to be formal — but I deliberately used Python throughout the course.

As it turns out, that is a pretty good way to stop yourself from writing all the code by hand instead of letting AI do the work.)))

My main job was not typing code. It was defining tasks, checking outputs, and paying attention to details.

Because every now and then, AI produces a solution best described as: **“Eh, good enough!”** 😁

That is when you need to notice the problem, explain what is wrong, and make sure it gets fixed.

In my view, programming experience is not a strict requirement for this AI course. It is more of a bonus.

The more important skills are expressing your thoughts clearly, defining tasks, verifying results, and being willing to experiment.

## What did the AI tools cost?

For this challenge, I managed with:

- **ChatGPT Plus — $20/month**
- **Groq — the free tier, with its limits**

I did not need additional paid API credits or a ChatGPT Pro subscription.

I used both cloud and local models. Another useful lesson was that building practical AI tools does not always require expensive subscriptions, powerful servers, or a huge budget.

## Thank you to the organizers

Thank you for the format, the hands-on assignments, and especially the weekly deadlines.

There were moments when I hated those deadlines.)))

But now I understand that without them, I would probably still be polishing an assignment from week five.

Instead of a completed challenge, I would have had one almost perfect solution.))

## The biggest takeaway

Over these seven weeks, I went from chatting with AI in a browser to building a local home assistant and practical agents that already do useful work.

But the most valuable outcome is not the tools themselves.

**I stopped seeing AI only as a conversation partner and started seeing it as a way to build my own systems.**

I now understand much better how these systems work, what they can do, and where human attention and judgment are essential.

So **if you can write, you are willing to experiment, and you can dedicate the next seven weeks to AI — welcome aboard!**

And please remember to warn your significant other.

**Seriously. I'm not joking!** 😂
